## Metal Gear Solid 5: Phantom Pain

My first encounter with this game was at a friend's place last year. The first thing I noticed when he showed me was the incredibly fast loading times, which is very rare these days. I've been playing video games for 20 years and I've seen the loading times slowly going up. The fact that MGSV loads very fast gives you a hint that the development team has spent a lot of time reducing it - so you know the game is good.

I've played through MGS2 a few times and I loved every second of it, but even though that game is such a classic I could not dare to have those expectations on this game. So I decided to not have any expectations at all. With a very intense start to this game you become interested in the story immediately, and it just keeps getting better. What I like most about it is that you're **constantly** being challenged in a creative way. Finding _challenging_ games can be tough if you're a very experienced gamer, but finding challenging _and_ expressive games is extremely rare.

So how does the game challenge you? For example, you are on a mission to infiltrate a base, save a prisoner and get the hell out of there. If you don't care about the guards noticing you, you can call in a helicopter, pop out your RPG and go bananas. That sentence alone captures a lot of action games I've played. It's fun, yes, but the fun lasts about 2 hours. This game encourages you to not get noticed by any guards and not to kill anyone. The optimal way, according to the game, would be to get to the prisoner, choke out the guards you find on your way there, extract the prisoner and then sneak back out again. Sounds easy? Not really.

As you make progress the game notices _how_ you take out the guards and counters your methods by giving the guards equipment such as helmets, flashlights, shotguns, body armor, shields etc. You can then counter _that_ counter by sending out your men to destroy the enemy's flashlight supply. See how complex it is? I love it.

My point is that you are challenged by this way of solving infiltrations, where you are not suppose to be seen. You have to use your tools, weapons and other items to fool the guards to be able to sneak pass them. There's a lot of ways to do it and I won't get into any of them here. This makes it a very creative experience, and sometimes you can't apply that well proven tactic you've been using for the last few missions, because of reasons, and you are forced to figure out something new. It makes you think, it tests your patience and you're gonna be ripping your hair sometimes. I LOVE IT!

This is the game mode you spend most of your time in, but slowly a new game mode is being born which you do not expect - maintaining Mother Base. This is all about management, managing people you "recruit" (you knock them out and pull them up in the sky with a balloon) and developing new weapons and items you can use during missions. But all of the sudden it becomes a lot more important to have a nice Mother Base when the online mode is introduced. For me it took about 50 hours for it to appear. So the game keeps throwing stuff at you, like "Oh, did you know you can also PLAY ME ONLINE, WITH OTHER HUMANS?!". What other game does that???

On top of all of this is the technical side of the game - the graphics, the sounds and the controls (!) which are also just amazing. Unfortunately no game is perfect, but what I don't like about this game is still no big thing. I am not sure why, but Snake does not talk a lot in the cut scenes in this game. There was a big fuzz about Kiefer Sutherland being the new voice actor for Snake in this game, but Kojima (the creator) didn't want to use his voice that much it seems like. This leads to Snake being rather weird from time to time, like someone asks him something and he doesn't reply. Maybe the studio wanted his body to express some sort of reply, but that is not always clear. Or maybe Snake has developed some sort of PTSD and has problems interacting with people - who knows.

I still give this game 10/10, and I recommend everyone who's up for a challenge to play this game. I'm about 40% (I know this because the game tells me) in and I've played around 50 hours. It's a lot of bang for your buck.
